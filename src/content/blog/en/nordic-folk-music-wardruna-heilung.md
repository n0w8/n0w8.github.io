**Nordic folk** is the genre of Wardruna (Norway, formed 2003) and Heilung (Copenhagen, formed 2014): newly composed music built on frame drums, lyres and horns and sung in Old Norse and other Nordic languages, which found a worldwide audience after Wardruna's founder Einar Selvik began working on the TV series *Vikings* in 2014. Search for Viking music today and those two names are hard to avoid. That is no accident, but it is not the whole story.

The genre is far younger than it sounds. Not a single melody written down in the Viking Age has survived; the earliest notated secular tune from Denmark sits in the Codex Runicus, a manuscript from around 1300. So whatever sounds like longships and sacrificial fires is, at best, an educated reconstruction. This guide maps the scene: who started it, which Nordic folk bands are worth your time, how the style differs from dark folk and Viking metal, and what order to listen in. For what we actually know about the music of the historical Norse, see our overview of [Viking music](/en/blog/viking-music/).

## What Is Nordic Folk Music?

"Nordic folk" is an umbrella label rather than a tightly defined genre. The same artists are also filed as Viking folk, ritual folk, pagan folk or dark folk. What they share is a recognisable core:

- **Historical and traditional instruments:** deer-hide frame drums, lyres, the bowed lyre known as the tagelharpa, mouth harp, goat horn, flutes, lurs and the Swedish nyckelharpa.
- **Old languages:** Old Norse above all, alongside Norwegian, Icelandic, Faroese and Danish, often with lyrics drawn from the Eddas or from runic inscriptions.
- **Ritual structure:** slow, pounding pulses, drones, chants and throat singing instead of verse and chorus.
- **Natural sound sources:** Wardruna also records trees, rocks, water and torches.

The word "folk" misleads a little: most of this is new composition that borrows old instruments and texts, not inherited village tradition. Which of those instruments can actually be traced to the Viking Age is a separate question, and we untangle it in our guide to [Viking instruments](/en/blog/viking-instruments/).

## How Viking Music Went Mainstream: Vikings, God of War and Valhalla

Television made the scene big. **Vikings** premiered on History in 2013 and ran for six seasons, with a score by Trevor Morris. In 2014 Einar Selvik announced that Wardruna would help compose the music for season two, and he later appeared on screen himself, playing a shaman in season three. Music by Danheim and fragments of Heilung were used in season six.

One twist: the title song, "If I Had a Heart", is by Fever Ray, the electronic project of Swedish artist Karin Dreijer, not by anyone from the folk scene. The "Viking sound" was a hybrid from episode one.

Still, the effect was real. Wardruna's third album, *Runaljod - Ragnarok*, came out in October 2016 and debuted at No. 1 on Billboard's World Albums chart, helped, as Wikipedia puts it, in part by the success of the show. How much of the show's hero is real is the subject of our piece on [Ragnar Lothbrok, legend and truth](/en/blog/ragnar-lothbrok-legend-truth/).

Video games picked up where television left off:

- **God of War (2018)** and **God of War Ragnarök (2022):** Bear McCreary's scores feature the voice of Faroese singer Eivør.
- **Assassin's Creed Valhalla (2020):** Einar Selvik worked on the soundtrack alongside Jesper Kyd and Sarah Schachner.
- **Senua's Saga: Hellblade II (2024):** the soundtrack was composed by Heilung.

Festivals did the rest: according to The New York Times, Heilung's 2017 shows at Midgardsblot in Norway made the band a name in the wider metal world.

## Wardruna and Einar Selvik: Where the Modern Sound Starts

If one name defines the modern scene, it is **Wardruna**. Einar Selvik founded the group in 2003 with Gaahl and Lindy-Fay Hella, and the founders' background is telling: Selvik was the drummer of Norwegian black metal band Gorgoroth from 2000 to 2004, and Gaahl came from the same band. The name Wardruna is usually glossed as "guardian of secrets" or "she who whispers".

Selvik, born in 1979, grew up on the island of Osterøy near Bergen and was drawn to pre-Christian culture as a teenager after finding an old book about runes. That thread runs straight into the **Runaljod trilogy**, which the band describes as an exploration of Norse myth, tradition and language through the 24 Proto-Norse runes, the Elder Futhark we break down sign by sign in our [Elder Futhark guide](/en/blog/elder-futhark-runes/):

1. **Runaljod - Gap var Ginnunga** (2009)
2. **Runaljod - Yggdrasil** (2013)
3. **Runaljod - Ragnarok** (2016)

The acoustic **Skald** followed in 2018, then **Kvitravn** in 2021 after the band signed with Sony Music and Columbia, and **Birna** in January 2025. Gaahl left on good terms in 2015. On stage, Selvik and his musicians play, among other things, the Kravik lyre, tagelharpa, goat horn, lur and moraharpa.

![Two Wardruna musicians blowing curved bronze lurs in front of a wall covered in giant shadows](/images/nordic-folk-music-2.jpg)

> Wardruna does not play historical Viking music. It writes new music with old tools, and says so, which sets it apart from many imitators.

## What Genre Is Heilung? "Amplified History" Explained

**Heilung** is usually filed under Nordic folk or neofolk, but the band calls its own music "amplified history from early medieval northern Europe". Its label, Season of Mist, lists it as "Amplified History / Neofolk".

Heilung formed in Copenhagen in 2014, with members from Denmark, Norway and Germany. At its core are German vocalist Kai Uwe Faust, Danish producer Christopher Juul and singer Maria Franz, who joined in 2015. According to The New York Times, the three met through the Viking reenactment scene. Their lyrics come partly from historical texts, including runic inscriptions on archaeological finds, and their sound sources include stones, bones and crude metal objects struck together. Live, the trio is surrounded by drummers, backing singers and performers dressed as warriors, as in the photo at the top.

Heilung says openly that it is not replicating ancestral practice, only offering an interpretation of how it might have felt. *Ofnir* (2015, first self-released) was followed by the live album *Lifa* (2017), *Futha* (2019) and *Drif* (2022). Our advice: see Heilung live if you can; the records carry only half of it.

## Bands Like Wardruna: 10 Nordic Folk Acts Compared

If Wardruna or Heilung hooked you, these are the next names to try, from quiet ritual to folk with metal roots.

| Danheim | Denmark | Ambient and Nordic folk | Munarvágr (2017) |

**Danheim** is the project of a Danish musician from Brøndby who made mostly electronic music until 2016, when an interest in Norse mythology changed his direction. His records have come out on his own label ever since, from *Munarvágr* (2017) through *Skapanir* (2020) to *Heimferd* (2025). In 2019 he contributed music to three episodes of the final season of *Vikings*.

**Myrkur** is the project of Danish singer Amalie Bruun, launched on Relapse Records in 2014 and first known for blending black metal with folk. With *Folkesange* (2020) she turned almost entirely to folk, with narrative ballads played on old instruments. The name is the Icelandic and Faroese word for "darkness".

![Faroese singer Eivør with an electric guitar, arms spread wide at the microphone](/images/nordic-folk-music-4.jpg)
*Eivør live at the Trianon in Paris, October 2024. Photo: Tilly antoine, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons*

**Eivør** Pálsdóttir, born on the Faroe Islands in 1983, is the most versatile voice here. Her records range from folk to jazz to electronica, sung in Faroese and English. Her voice reached a far wider audience through the God of War soundtracks and the music for *The Last Kingdom*. In 2021 she won the Nordic Council Music Prize.

**SKÁLD** is a French group founded in 2018 by producer Christophe Voisin-Boisvinet. They sing mostly in Old Norse, and the lyrics of their debut *Vikings Chant* (Decca, January 2019) come mainly from the Poetic Edda. The line-up has changed several times since, but the lyre, talharpa and nyckelharpa have stayed.

**Faun** predates Wardruna. The German band formed in 1998 and coined the term "pagan folk" itself, first to describe its electronically amplified concerts. It mixes medieval and folk melodies with electronics and sings in German, Middle High German, Old Norse and more. *Midgard* (2016) reached No. 3 on the German album chart, and Einar Selvik has recorded with the band.

**Hagalaz' Runedance** was the project of German musician Andrea Haugen (1969-2021) from 1996 to 2002. After moving to Norway she immersed herself in Nordic, Celtic and medieval musical tradition. We count it among the early forerunners of today's scene, years before anyone used the phrase Nordic folk.

**Kati Rán** is a Dutch musician who plays historical instruments such as the nyckelharpa and moraharpa and sings in Scandinavian languages and Old Norse. The songs on her album *SÁLA* (2024) are named after the nine mythological daughters of the sea goddess Rán, with guests including Heilung and Gaahl. Her screen credits include *Vikings: Valhalla*.

**Nytt Land** is a duo from Kalachinsk in Siberia, founded in 2013 by husband and wife Anatoly and Natalya Pakhalenko. Album titles such as *Havamal* (2015) and *Scopun: Songs from the Elder Edda* (2016) show the direction, and their style has been compared to Wardruna and Danheim. *Ritual* (2021) came out on Napalm Records.

## Nordwald Records: Eldruna, Domsgard, Skaedra and Rúnvyr

A disclosure first: Nordwald Records belongs to the same company as this blog. So we describe its four projects plainly, by style, language and songs. According to the label, all four are produced in Austria and brought to life with modern digital tools. Profiles of all four are on the [Nordwald Records](https://www.nordwaldrecords.com/) site.

### Eldruna

The label files [Eldruna](https://eldruna.com/) under Viking and Nordic folk: a raw, smoky female voice over war drums, singing about wolves, oaths and the sea in a mix of old and modern languages. The debut album *Mørke* came out in February 2026, followed by *Ulvens Pagt* and the paired albums *The Good.* and *The Bad.*, among others. The most-watched videos include "Mama, Don't Cry", "Brother Hold Me" and "Stay Strong".

### Domsgard

[Domsgard](https://domsgard.com/) is the oldest project in the catalogue; its first release, *Ulfar i skugga*, came out in July 2025. ### Skaedra

[Skaedra](https://skaedra.com/) is also listed as Viking and Nordic folk, sung in English and Old Norse. The songs are built from frame drums, bone percussion and low drones, and they are less about heroes than about what heroes leave behind: long journeys home, the cold, and names nobody wrote down, the idea behind the Old Norse word orðstírr. The album *The First Raid* (June 2026) was followed by *Unforgotten*; the best-known tracks are "Blodkald" (with Eldruna), "Storm Chant" and "Queen of Blood".

### Rúnvyr

[Rúnvyr](https://runvyrmusic.com/) is listed as Nordic dark folk and sings in Danish, English and German. Tribal drums, cold soundscapes and emotional vocals carry songs from war chants to quiet ballads about loss, often drawing on Danish history from the Danevirke and Hedeby to the Kattegat. Well-known tracks include "Mor i Stormen", "Bær Mig Hjem" and "Svartravn" (with Domsgard), and the albums include *Danmark* (September 2026).

## Nordic Folk vs Dark Folk vs Viking Metal

The labels blur, but four rough boxes help:

- **Nordic folk (or Viking folk):** historical instruments, old languages, mostly acoustic, often slow and ritualistic. Think Wardruna, SKÁLD, Danheim.
- **Neofolk and dark folk:** neofolk emerged in 1980s England out of the post-industrial scene, with bands such as Death In June, Current 93 and Sol Invictus It is mostly acoustic and sombre. "Dark folk" is now used much more loosely, for artists like Kati Rán or Rúnvyr.
- **Pagan folk:** the term Faun coined. Medieval and folk melodies, often with electronics, and a mythological range that goes well beyond the North.
- **Viking metal:** grew out of black metal. Sweden's Bathory is generally credited with founding it on *Blood Fire Death* (1988) and *Hammerheart* (1990), with Norway's Enslaved as another formative band. Guitars and drums lead; folk instruments play a smaller role than in folk metal.

The borders are porous. Wardruna plays no metal at all, yet black metal fans embraced the band, which is less surprising once you remember where its founders came from. Myrkur travelled the other way, from metal into folk.

## Where to Start: A Listening Order for Beginners

Our suggestion, from quiet to crushing:

1. **Wardruna - Skald (2018):** acoustic and stripped back, showing how much one voice and a few instruments can carry.
2. **Wardruna - the Runaljod trilogy (2009-2016):** the core of the genre, best heard in release order.
3. **Heilung - Lifa (2017):** a live album, because live is where Heilung makes sense. Then *Ofnir*.
4. **Danheim or SKÁLD:** more melodic, more rhythmic and easier to get into.
5. **Eivør and Myrkur:** for anyone who wants more song and less ritual.

If you still want guitars after that, Bathory and Enslaved are the doorway into Viking metal.

## FAQ

### What bands are like Wardruna?

The closest matches are Heilung, Danheim, SKÁLD, Kati Rán and Nytt Land, all of whom work with historical instruments and old Nordic languages. Eivør and Myrkur are more song-based, while Faun sounds more medieval and more electronic.

### What genre is Heilung?

Heilung is usually classed as Nordic folk or neofolk. The band itself calls its music "amplified history from early medieval northern Europe", and its label Season of Mist lists it as "Amplified History / Neofolk".

### What is the difference between Nordic folk and Viking metal?

Nordic folk is built on historical instruments such as drums, lyres and horns and is mostly acoustic. Viking metal grew out of black metal, took shape as its own style with Bathory's albums of 1988 and 1990, and is driven by electric guitars and drums. Both draw on the Viking Age and Norse mythology for their themes.

### Is Nordic folk real Viking music?

No. No melody written down in the Viking Age survives, and Denmark's oldest notated secular tune comes from the Codex Runicus of around 1300. Nordic folk is modern music that uses old instruments, texts and sound ideas, and the leading bands, Wardruna and Heilung included, say so openly.

### What music is used in the Vikings TV series?

The score is by Trevor Morris, with Wardruna contributing from season two. Season six also used music by Danheim and fragments of Heilung. The title song, "If I Had a Heart", is by Fever Ray, an electronic project from Sweden.

## Our Take: New Music, Old Tools

Nordic folk owes part of its success to a misunderstanding, one its best artists are happy to correct. Many listeners arrive via *Vikings*, God of War or Assassin's Creed believing they are hearing what the Vikings heard. What they are really hearing is a modern art form that works with runes, Eddic verse and reconstructed instruments, a bit like a historical film that comes with footnotes.

That is what makes it worth exploring: Wardruna and Heilung build something new on a respectful reading of the sources, without pretending to be time travellers. If you want the historical side, start with our pieces on [Viking music](/en/blog/viking-music/) and [Viking instruments](/en/blog/viking-instruments/). Otherwise: headphones on, start with *Skald* and let the North pull you in from there.
